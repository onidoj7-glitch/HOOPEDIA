Basketball Rules

Basketball rules define how teams move the ball, score, and compete fairly. Exact details vary by league and level of play.

On this page

  1. Basic rules
  2. Scoring
  3. Fouls and violations

Basic rules

Two teams play on a court with a basket at each end. The aim is to score more points than the opposing team before playing time ends. A team usually has five players on the court at a time in standard full-court basketball.

  • Players advance the ball by passing or dribbling. A dribble is a repeated bounce of the ball with one hand.
  • A player who has stopped dribbling generally cannot begin another dribble while still holding the ball; doing so is a double-dribble violation.
  • Players may not run while holding the ball without dribbling. Illegal movement of the feet while holding the ball is called traveling.
  • The ball is out of bounds when it touches a boundary line, the floor outside the court, or a person or object outside the court. The other team usually receives possession.
  • Games use timed periods, and the team with more points when regulation time ends wins. Some competitions use overtime if the score is tied.

Clock rules, court dimensions, period lengths, and some violations differ among competitions such as FIBA, the NBA, and school leagues.

Scoring

The value of a made basket depends on where and how the shot is taken. A free throw is an uncontested attempt awarded after certain fouls.

Common scoring values
PointsHow they are scored
1A made free throw.
2A field goal made from inside the three-point line.
3A field goal made from beyond the three-point line, with the shooter behind the line at takeoff.

The three-point line distance is set by the competition and may differ across leagues or court levels.

Fouls and violations

A violation is a rule breach that usually results in the other team taking the ball. A foul generally involves illegal contact or unsporting conduct and may also lead to free throws, depending on the situation and ruleset.

Common violations

  • Traveling: illegally moving the feet while holding the ball.
  • Double dribble: starting a second dribble after ending the first, or dribbling with both hands at once.
  • Out of bounds: touching the ball while a player or the ball is out of bounds.
  • Backcourt: in leagues with a backcourt rule, a team that has established the ball in the frontcourt cannot illegally return it to the backcourt.
  • Goaltending: illegally interfering with a shot on its downward path toward the basket or with a ball above the rim, as defined by the rules.

Common fouls

Personal fouls include illegal contact such as holding, pushing, or impeding an opponent. A blocking foul can be called when a defender has not established a legal guarding position; a charge is an offensive foul for illegally running into a legally positioned defender in qualifying circumstances. Technical and unsportsmanlike fouls address conduct or contact covered by each competition's rules.

Foul limits and free-throw penalties are competition-specific. See also defensive strategy and defensive fundamentals.
