Plays and Techniques

Basketball plays coordinate players' movement to create scoring chances or defend the basket. Techniques are the individual skills used to carry them out.

Common plays

Teams adapt their plays to their players, opponents, and the time and score. A play is a shared plan, not a guaranteed result.

Pick and roll

One offensive player sets a screen for a teammate handling the ball, then moves toward the basket or into open space. The ball handler can attack, pass to the screener, or find another teammate if the defense helps.

Give and go

A player passes to a teammate and immediately cuts toward an open area, often near the basket, looking for a return pass.

Fast break

After gaining possession, a team advances quickly before the defense is fully set. Players run into lanes, and the ball handler looks for a safe pass or open path to score.

Backdoor cut

An offensive player moves away from the ball as if to receive it, then cuts behind an overplaying defender toward the basket for a pass.

Dribbling

Dribbling lets a player move with the ball. Players use controlled, repeated bounces and keep their body between the ball and a defender when needed. Practicing with both hands, changing pace, and keeping the head up can help with control and awareness.

Passing

Passing moves the ball between teammates, often faster than dribbling. A chest pass travels directly from the passer's chest; a bounce pass strikes the floor before reaching a teammate; an overhead pass can clear a defender. Good passes are timed, accurate, and easy for a teammate to catch.

Shooting

Shooting is an attempt to score by sending the ball through the basket. A balanced stance, controlled release, and follow-through are common fundamentals. Players practice close shots, layups, free throws, and longer-range shots while learning to select a shot based on position and defensive pressure.

Rebounding

A rebound is gained after a missed field-goal or free-throw attempt. On defense, players locate an opponent and establish space before pursuing the ball; this is commonly called boxing out. An offensive rebound gives the shooting team another possession.

Defense

Individual defense relies on balanced footwork, awareness, and staying between an opponent and the basket without illegal contact. Team defense also requires communication, help positioning, and recovering to open players. Learn more about defensive strategies.
